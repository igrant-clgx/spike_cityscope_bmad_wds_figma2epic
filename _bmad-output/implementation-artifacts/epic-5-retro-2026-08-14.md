## 2. What Went Well

- **The port + envelope seam reproduced a fifth time — the template is now invisible infrastructure.** `LeadSink` was built by copying the `AddressProvider`/`ConfigSource`/`EstimateEngine` shape: stub adapter, BFF route re-validating input AND output, AD-9 envelope, non-throwing `apiFetch` (`data.ok === false`), TanStack Query mutation ownership. Story 5.1's design cost was near-zero and 5.2–5.4 consumed the seam without re-litigation. Five adapters, one template — the pipeline-fidelity thesis holding at full strength through the epic that finally crosses a real trust boundary.
- **The boundary arch test earned its keep on the highest-stakes adapter — automatically.** `LeadSink` is the first adapter to land *after* the arch test became machine-enforced (Epic 4), and it auto-walked the new adapter with zero new wiring. The critical AD-1 decision — keeping the idempotency key as transport metadata *off* the `LeadCapture` PII interface and the Zod body — was made and held under a test that would fail on any inward `next`/`react`/`zod` import. The debt paid down in Epic 4 protected the epic where it mattered most.
- **PII confinement held under adversarial pressure — the #1 carried risk closed cleanly.** Every lead story was reviewed explicitly for PII leakage to analytics, logs, query-strings, and `NEXT_PUBLIC`. `lead_submitted` carries only `leadId` + contact-method *category*, protected by a compile-time `AssertNoPII` type guard AND a runtime `FORBIDDEN_PII_KEYS` test that fails if `firstName`/`email`/`phone` are ever added. Both reviewers independently re-verified it clean on 5.4. Epic 4 Action Item #1 (enforce BFF-only PII) is discharged with a real test, not just a checklist line.
- **The consent gate was made explicit and tested — and ordered correctly against dedup.** AD-10 (no consent → no store) ships as a unit-tested gate in the stub sink, and — crucially — the gate is checked BEFORE the idempotency ledger, so a consent-less retry can never short-circuit to a fake success by matching a prior key. Both reviewers confirmed the ordering. Epic 4 Action Item #2 discharged.
- **AU validators landed as pure, exhaustively-tested helpers — and the reviewers hardened them further.** `isAuPhone`/`isAuEmail` and the `leadFormFieldErrors`/`isLeadFormSubmittable`/`toLeadRequestFields` gate are pure functions with node-only coverage (Epic 4 Action Item #4). The reviewer loop then tightened real edge cases the first cut missed: email dot-placement (`.jane@`, `a..b@`, `jane@example..com`, trailing dot) and phone separator stripping (`-()。.`). Pure-function extraction kept the AU-format minefield fully testable without a browser.
- **The two-parallel-reviewer loop caught a HIGH in three of four stories — every one an invisible correctness defect.** 5.1 the per-instance counter collision, 5.3 the `''`/`undefined` schema contract drift, 5.4 the cross-user idempotency-key collision. None is visible to typecheck/lint/build/test; each is a genuine data-integrity defect (duplicate/colliding lead identity, or a schema-rejected payload). The loop is now the load-bearing gate for exactly the class of bug the gates cannot see.

## 3. What Was Challenging

- **Idempotency identity was subtly wrong twice — for the same underlying reason.** Both 5.1 (`leadId` disambiguation counter scoped per-sink-instance while the route builds a fresh sink per request) and 5.4 (idempotency key built from a *deterministic* `estimateId` + a *per-tab* client counter, matched against a *process-wide* ledger) were failures of **scope reasoning**: a value that looked unique in one scope collided in the scope that actually mattered. `estimateId` was deliberately built order-independent and 64-bit in Epic 4 to be a durable join key — the trap in 5.4 was that its very determinism (two users, same items, same id) made it *wrong* as idempotency entropy. Lesson: idempotency keys need real per-request entropy (`crypto.randomUUID()`), never a content hash or a client-bundle counter, whenever the dedup store is shared across users/processes.
- **The `''` vs `undefined` contract seam between the client gate and the Zod schema drifted silently.** The client form modeled an unselected optional field as `''`; the schema's `.optional()` accepts `undefined`, not `''`. Typecheck passed (both are strings at the form layer) but the payload would have been schema-rejected at the boundary. It took the reviewer loop cross-checking the gate output against `safeParse` to surface it. Client-form value shapes and server-schema value shapes are two contracts that must be reconciled by an explicit adapter (`toLeadRequestFields`), not assumed congruent.
- **The "same-commit / competing live region" a11y trap recurred a *fifth* time — now as two panels each owning a region.** In the success state, ResultsPanel's persistent `role="status"` region and LeadPanel's own region are both on screen. LeadPanel's is empty at rest so both reviewers rated it tolerable, but it is the same class of defect (which region speaks, and is there exactly one) that has appeared in every epic. SR-announcement correctness remains the one class the node-only harness cannot assert; it is deferred into the Epic 6 a11y audit rather than pretended solved.
- **Stateful-mutation retry semantics are genuinely harder than the read/compute seams.** Every prior adapter was idempotent-by-nature (address lookup, config read, deterministic estimate). The lead POST is the first *stateful* mutation, which forced net-new design: `maxRetries: 0` (never auto-retry a write), a stable idempotency key across manual retries, a module-level dedup ledger, consent-before-dedup ordering, and once-only analytics across an error→success transition. Several of these (empty-header key handling, retry double-click race, unbounded ledger growth) surfaced only under adversarial tracing. The seam template covered the *shape*; the *statefulness* needed real care the read seams never did.

## 5. Next Epic Preview — Epic 6: Release Readiness & Verification

**Goal:** Prove the assembled product is release-ready — not by adding features, but by **verifying** the whole surface against its non-functional contracts. This is the pipeline-fidelity spike's closing argument: does the end-to-end build hold up under systematic screen-state, accessibility, responsive/performance, security/privacy, and cross-browser scrutiny?

**Stories (verification-focused, may be more audit/documentation than net-new code):**
- **6.1** Screen-state matrix sign-off — enumerate and verify every screen × state (loading/empty/success/low-confidence/error) across the whole flow.
- **6.2** Accessibility audit to WCAG 2.1 AA — the home for every deferred SR-only-correctness item (competing live regions, post-action focus management, lead-form announcements).
- **6.3** Responsive & performance verification — breakpoints, layout integrity, and performance budgets.
- **6.4** Security / privacy / reliability / observability review — the AD-1/AD-10/NFR-6 posture verified end-to-end, plus the deferred OI-11 real-sink concerns (unbounded idempotency ledger, encryption-at-rest) framed for a real backend.
- **6.5** Test-suite & browser-matrix verification — coverage assessment and cross-browser confirmation.

**Dependencies on Epics 1–5 (all satisfied):**
- The full feature surface (address → scope → estimate → lead) is built and green (522 tests, 70 files).
- The machine-enforced boundary arch test, the PII guards, and the consent gate give 6.4 concrete, already-tested artifacts to audit rather than build.
- The four documented state machines (`toResultsView`, `toLeadView`, and the form/results/lead surfaces) give 6.1 a ready enumeration basis.
- `deferred-work.md` is the running agenda for 6.2 (a11y items) and 6.4 (OI-11 real-sink hardening).

**Preparation / risks carried into Epic 6:**
1. **Adapt the dev-auto loop to verification work.** Epic 6 stories are audits, not features — the spec → implement → review → finalize loop still applies, but "implement" may mean producing a verified matrix/audit document + any small remediation, and the reviewer loop shifts from "find the injected bug" to "confirm the audit is complete and honest." Don't force net-new code where verification is the deliverable.
2. **SR-only correctness finally gets its dedicated pass (6.2).** Every deferred a11y item — competing live regions (5.4), post-action focus (4.4), lead-form focus/announcements — converges here. This is the one class the node-only harness cannot assert; 6.2 is where the documented manual a11y pass becomes a first-class artifact.
3. **The node-only test harness bounds what 6.3/6.5 can assert.** No jsdom/RTL, no browser — responsive/performance/browser-matrix verification will lean on static analysis, build output, and documented manual checks rather than automated in-browser assertions. Set that expectation in the specs; don't add test deps to fake it.
4. **OI-11 (real `LeadSink`) framing in 6.4.** The unbounded idempotency ledger and encryption-at-rest stub markings are honest spike shortcuts — 6.4 should frame the real-backend requirements (bounded/TTL dedup store, actual encryption) as the substitution the seam already isolates, not re-open them as spike work.

## 7. Pipeline-Fidelity Verdict

Epic 5 took the methodology across its first real trust boundary — consented PII, a stateful mutation, an external-lead conversion — and the intent survived intact. The `LeadSink` port reproduced the now-canonical seam a fifth time at near-zero design cost, and for the first time landed under a boundary arch test that was itself paid-down debt from the prior epic, protecting the AD-1 decision precisely where it mattered most. The two highest-risk items in the entire spike — BFF-only PII and a tested consent gate — closed with real machine-enforced guards rather than checklist promises. Where drift appeared it was, once again, **mechanical rather than conceptual**: two idempotency-scope miscalculations, a `''`/`undefined` schema seam, a fifth competing-live-region recurrence — every one caught by the pure-logic suite plus the two-parallel-reviewer loop, three of four stories surfacing a genuine HIGH that no gate could see. The stateful mutation proved harder than the read seams, exactly as predicted, and the SR-only-correctness ceiling remains the one honest limit of the node-only harness — now routed, deliberately, into a dedicated Epic 6 a11y audit rather than deferred into fog. Five epics in, the seam is a reflex, the reviewer loop is the load-bearing correctness gate, and the pipeline is not merely holding across a trust boundary — it is compounding its own guarantees.
