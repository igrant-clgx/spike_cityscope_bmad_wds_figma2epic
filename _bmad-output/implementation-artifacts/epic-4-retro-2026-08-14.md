## 2. What Went Well

- **The port + envelope seam reproduced a fourth time — now a reflex, not a decision.** `EstimateEngine` was built by copying the `AddressProvider`/`ConfigSource` shape: stub adapter, BFF route, AD-9 envelope, non-throwing `apiFetch` (`data.ok === false`), TanStack Query ownership (a **mutation** this time, cleanly). Story 4.1's design cost was near-zero and Stories 4.2–4.4 consumed the seam without re-litigation. Four adapters, one template — the pipeline-fidelity thesis at full strength.
- **The long-carried boundary arch test finally landed — and immediately earned its keep.** After being deferred across three epics, `src/server/architecture.test.ts` shipped as the first task of 4.1. The adversarial reviewer then proved the *first* implementation scanned line-by-line and missed every multiline import — so the test that existed to retire a manual grep would itself have passed a real violation. The rewrite (strip comments + string/template literals, scan the whole file with multiline-aware regexes, self-check on synthetic sources) machine-enforces layer purity for the two remaining adapters (`LeadSink` in Epic 5) that grep would have guarded on borrowed time.
- **AD-7 integer-cents money held with zero float drift.** `AudCents` (branded, `audCents()` guard throws on non-safe-int) runs through domain, adapter, and envelope; the cents→AUD conversion exists only at the view edge in a pure, exhaustively-tested `formatAud`/`formatAudRange` (boundaries, honest `Math.round(cents/100)`, finite-guard, ordered range args). The classic off-by-100 / float-money defects never appeared because the type made them unrepresentable in the core.
- **The two-parallel-reviewer loop caught both MED defects the gates could not — again invisible a11y correctness.** Story 4.3: both reviewers surfaced that the card carried its *own* `aria-live` region competing with the persistent panel region, so the reliable region announced only a generic "ready" while the actual dollar figure sat in a freshly-mounted region SRs announce inconsistently — the exact same-commit-live-region failure mode. Story 4.2: a duplicate auto-generated region id (invalid HTML). Neither is visible to typecheck/lint/build/test.
- **Two long-open product/process debts closed this epic.** OI-7 (reset scope) was reconciled to a *signed* clear-all decision in the 4.4 spec before wiring Edit/New — the first time that carried item was resolved rather than re-deferred. The persistent-live-region contract deferred from 4.2 was genuinely discharged in 4.3 (a single panel-owned region). Deferrals that pay down rather than accrue.

## 3. What Was Challenging

- **The test that was supposed to end a blind spot had the blind spot.** The boundary arch test's first cut scanned line-by-line — so multiline `import {\n ... \n} from 'next'` slipped straight through. The action item had been carried for three epics precisely to catch inward imports, and the naive implementation would have shipped a green test over a real violation. It took an adversarial reviewer rendering a synthetic multiline import to catch it. Lesson: an enforcement test needs its own adversarial proof (a known-bad input it must reject), or it's security theatre.
- **The "same-commit live region" a11y trap recurred in a new shape.** Epic 4 hit the live-region announcement problem twice: the card owning a second region (4.3) and the arrival announcement timing (deferred from 4.2). Screen-reader announcement correctness — *which* region speaks and *when* — remains a class the node-only harness cannot assert; it rests on the reviewer loop plus a documented manual a11y pass. This is the fourth epic in a row where SR-only correctness needed human/adversarial verification.
- **`estimateId` identity needed real care to be a durable join key.** The stub is deterministic, but the id has to be **order-independent** (item selection order must not change it), **deduped**, **stable for empty scope**, and wide enough to avoid collisions across the lead-linkage space Epic 5 will build on. The first cut was a 32-bit hash (collision risk over the join space) — widened to a 2-lane FNV-1a 64-bit hex. Getting an "obvious" hash right for its downstream contract was non-trivial.
- **MUI v9 SSR quirks kept taxing — now on the results surface.** MUI auto-generates the Accordion region id from the summary's `aria-controls` (so a hand-set id on `AccordionDetails` duplicates it); `Collapse` defaults `unmountOnExit:false` (collapsed bodies ARE emitted in `renderToStaticMarkup`, making some expander/reveal assertions true-green regardless of state); `Stack`/`Typography` reject `alignItems`/`textAlign` as direct props. Each cost a debugging cycle, and one produced a genuinely hollow reduced-motion test (asserting content presence a `Collapse` always emits) that a reviewer flagged and we re-pointed at the actual motion signal.

## 5. Next Epic Preview — Epic 5: Lead Capture — Talk to a Home Loan Coach

**Goal:** Convert delivered value into a **consented financing lead** linked to the current `estimateId` via the `LeadSink` port (stub store for the spike): a low-pressure "Talk to a Home Loan Coach" CTA + phone contact, a validated lead form (AU phone/email formats), an explicit consent gate, and submission with a success confirmation.

**FRs covered:** FR-26 through FR-31 · **NFR:** NFR-6 (privacy) · **AD:** AD-1 (BFF-only PII), AD-4 (shared lead schema), AD-10 (consent-gated + encrypted-at-rest lead) · **UX-DR:** 11 (contact section / phone CTA), 12 (lead form), 16 (lead-surface states), 17 (low-pressure CTA), 20 (lead a11y).

**Stories:** 5.1 LeadSink port, stub store & shared lead schema · 5.2 Contact Section & Coach CTA · 5.3 Lead form with AU validation & consent gate · 5.4 Submit lead linked to estimate with confirmation & states.

**Dependencies on Epics 1–4 (all satisfied):**
- The port + BFF + AD-9 envelope pattern — `LeadSink` follows the exact `AddressProvider`/`ConfigSource`/`EstimateEngine` shape (a four-times-proven template) and is now guarded by the boundary arch test.
- TanStack Query async ownership (AD-5) — lead submit reuses the mutation pattern established for the estimate (loading + duplicate-submit prevention + non-destructive error/retry).
- The stable `estimateId` (Story 4.1 / AD-9) — the lead record links to it; the identity was built order-independent and 64-bit precisely for this join.
- Shared Zod schema conventions (AD-4) + `FormTextField`/rhf form patterns (Epic 3) + the results/lead-surface state machine (Epic 4's `toResultsView` is the template for the lead-surface states).

**Preparation / risks carried into Epic 5:**
1. **PII crosses a real trust boundary for the first time (AD-1 / NFR-6).** Lead PII must flow **only** through the BFF, never client-to-external. The boundary arch test guards *import* direction, not data flow — add an explicit review check (and, if cheap, a test) that the client never posts PII anywhere but the same-origin BFF route.
2. **AD-10 consent gate + encryption-at-rest marking.** Storage must be consent-gated (no consent → no store) and the stored record marked for encryption. For the spike this is a stub store, but the *gate* and the *marking* are the intent under test — make them explicit and tested, not implied.
3. **AU-format validation (FR-28, NFR-10).** AU phone/email formats with inline errors — extract the validators as pure, exhaustively-tested helpers (the Epic 3 `validateAnswer` pattern); AU phone formats are a classic edge-case minefield (mobile vs landline, spaces, +61).
4. **`[OPEN]` OI-10 — conversion path (phone CTA vs lead form).** Story 5.2 ships the phone CTA and flags OI-10; Story 5.3's form is gated on the OI-10 resolution. Surface it for a product decision early so 5.3 isn't blocked.
5. **Lead-surface states + a11y (UX-DR16/UX-DR20 lead).** Submit loading + duplicate-submit prevention, validation errors, success confirmation, submit error + retry preserving entered data — reuse Epic 4's `toResultsView` state-mapper template. Consent semantics + labelled fields re-open the SR-only correctness surface — plan the manual a11y check up front.
6. **Post-action focus management (deferred from 4.4).** The focus-relocation gap after Edit/New (focus falls to `<body>`) sits in the same manual-a11y bucket as the lead-form focus/announcement work — fold it into Epic 5's manual a11y pass.

## 7. Pipeline-Fidelity Verdict

Epic 4 delivered the value moment with intent intact. The `EstimateEngine` port reproduced the now-canonical seam a fourth time at near-zero design cost; AD-7's integer-cents type made float-money defects unrepresentable in the core; the `estimateId` was shaped deliberately for the Epic 5 join it doesn't yet have. Most tellingly, the epic **paid down its two oldest debts** — the boundary arch test (carried three epics, now machine-enforced) and OI-7 (now a signed decision) — rather than rolling them forward again. Where drift appeared it was **mechanical, not conceptual**: a line-by-line arch test that missed multiline imports, two competing live regions, a 32-bit id, a hollow motion assertion — every one caught by the pure-logic suite plus the adversarial reviewer loop, none by luck. The recurring SR-only-correctness ceiling remains the one genuinely hard limit of the node-only harness, and it is honestly bounded by the reviewer loop and a documented manual a11y pass rather than pretended away. Four epics in, the methodology is not just holding — it is compounding.
